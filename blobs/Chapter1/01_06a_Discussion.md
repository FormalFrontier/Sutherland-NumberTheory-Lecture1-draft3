## 1.3 Absolute values on $\mathbf{Q}$

To avoid confusion we will denote the usual absolute value on $\mathbf{Q}$ (inherited from $\mathbf{R}$) by $|\cdot|_\infty$; it is an archimedean absolute value. But there are are infinitely many others. Recall that any element of $\mathbf{Q}^\times$ may be written as $\pm \prod_q q^{e_q}$, where the product ranges over primes and the exponents $e_q \in \mathbf{Z}$ are uniquely determined (as is the sign).
